# Monetization

Monetization connects customer value to durable revenue. It includes the value metric, packaging, price, billing model, payment execution, and the experiments used to improve conversion and retention.

## Monetization sequence

1. **Identify the valuable outcome.** State what improves for the customer and how they measure that improvement.
2. **Choose a value metric.** Charge along a dimension that grows with customer value—seats, usage, transactions, assets, contacts, or another defensible unit.
3. **Segment willingness to pay.** Interview distinct customer groups about alternatives, budgets, purchasing authority, urgency, and perceived risk.
4. **Design packaging.** Separate plans by customer need and sophistication, not by randomly withholding features.
5. **Set the price architecture.** Choose free trial, freemium, flat subscription, per-seat, tiered usage, credits, transaction fee, outcome-based, or a hybrid.
6. **Model the economics.** Test gross margin, cost to serve, acquisition cost, payback, retention, expansion, support load, and cash timing.
7. **Build the purchase path.** Make pricing, limits, taxes, checkout, invoicing, payment recovery, upgrades, downgrades, and cancellation understandable.
8. **Instrument the funnel.** Measure exposure, intent, checkout, payment success, activation, retention, expansion, contraction, and churn.
9. **Experiment safely.** Change one major pricing or packaging assumption at a time, segment results, and protect existing customer commitments.

## Core decisions

| Decision | Key question |
| --- | --- |
| Value metric | Does the bill grow when customer value grows? |
| Packaging | Can each target segment recognize the right plan quickly? |
| Entry model | Does free access accelerate learning or create service cost without intent? |
| Price level | Is the price anchored to economic value and credible alternatives? |
| Billing model | Is revenue predictable without making usage feel risky? |
| Expansion | Is there a natural path to more usage, seats, products, or outcomes? |

## Revenue dashboard

- Visitor-to-signup and signup-to-activation conversion
- Free-to-paid or trial-to-paid conversion
- Checkout completion and payment authorization rate
- Monthly and annual recurring revenue
- New, expansion, contraction, reactivation, and churned revenue
- Gross revenue retention and net revenue retention
- Logo churn and revenue churn by segment and cohort
- Average revenue per account and lifetime value
- Customer acquisition cost and CAC payback period
- Gross margin and cost to serve by pricing plan

## Guardrails

- Do not optimize checkout conversion before customers consistently reach value.
- Do not copy a competitor's price without understanding its segment and economics.
- Do not use usage pricing when customers cannot predict or control usage.
- Do not hide essential limits or make cancellation intentionally difficult.
- Treat pricing as a product system requiring research, design, engineering, finance, and support—not a one-time number.
