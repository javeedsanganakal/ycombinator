# Sales

A practical system for turning a validated customer problem into repeatable revenue. Early-stage founders should do the first sales themselves: the goal is to learn why customers buy before hiring a team to repeat the process.

## Sales path

1. **Choose the customer.** Define an ideal customer profile using observable traits: industry, company size, role, workflow, trigger event, current alternative, and ability to pay.
2. **Write the problem hypothesis.** Describe the painful job, its frequency, business impact, and the evidence that the buyer already spends time or money on it.
3. **Build a target list.** Start narrow. Select 50–100 accounts that fit the hypothesis rather than collecting thousands of weak leads.
4. **Run discovery.** Ask about the current workflow, consequences, prior attempts, decision process, urgency, and budget. Do not turn discovery into a feature presentation.
5. **Qualify.** Confirm pain, authority, urgency, fit, and a concrete next step. Disqualifying weak opportunities keeps the pipeline honest.
6. **Demonstrate value.** Show the shortest path from the customer's problem to a measurable outcome. Tailor the demo to what was learned in discovery.
7. **Close explicitly.** Agree on scope, price, stakeholders, security or legal review, decision date, and implementation plan.
8. **Deliver and retain.** Track activation, realized value, adoption, support signals, renewal risk, expansion, and referrals.
9. **Make it repeatable.** Document messages, objections, stages, exit criteria, conversion rates, cycle time, and reasons for losses before hiring salespeople.

## Minimum pipeline

```text
Target account → Contacted → Discovery → Qualified → Evaluation
               → Proposal → Commit → Won/Lost → Activated → Renewed/Expanded
```

Every stage needs a customer action as its exit criterion. A sent email is activity, not progress.

## Metrics that matter

| Metric | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Positive reply rate | Tests whether targeting and messaging resonate |
| Discovery-to-qualified rate | Tests whether the chosen segment has the problem |
| Qualified-to-won rate | Tests offer, trust, product fit, and sales execution |
| Sales-cycle length | Shows how quickly revenue can become predictable |
| Average contract value | Connects the sales motion to viable acquisition cost |
| Activation/time-to-value | Tests whether closed customers reach the promised outcome |
| Gross and net revenue retention | Reveals whether revenue persists and expands |
| Loss reasons | Directs product, positioning, pricing, and qualification changes |

## Working areas

- Ideal customer profile and segmentation
- Customer and buyer research
- Founder-led outbound and inbound qualification
- Discovery interviews and demos
- CRM and pipeline design
- Proposals, negotiation, procurement, and closing
- Customer success, renewal, and expansion
- Sales hiring, compensation, enablement, and forecasting
