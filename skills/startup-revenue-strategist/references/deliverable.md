# Product-to-Revenue Plan

Use this structure when the user supplies a product and asks for a comprehensive plan. Scale detail to the available evidence and stage, but do not omit a section silently.

## 1. Executive diagnosis

In one screen state:

- Product and customer outcome.
- Revenue stage and commercial motion.
- Primary constraint.
- Recommended ICP.
- Recommended position and monetization model.
- Primary acquisition/sales channel.
- Three highest-priority actions.
- Confidence and biggest unknown.

## 2. Evidence and assumptions

Use a table:

| Claim | Status | Evidence/source | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Status must be `verified`, `user-provided`, `inferred`, or `unknown`. Cite current external facts near the claim.

## 3. Product truth

Provide:

- Plain-language one-sentence product description.
- User, buyer, and other stakeholders.
- Painful job and trigger event.
- Current alternatives, including manual work/inaction.
- Promised outcome and time-to-value.
- Existing proof and missing proof.
- Activation and retained-value behaviors.

## 4. Segment and ICP

Compare plausible segments, then select one starting segment:

| Segment | Pain/urgency | Reachability | Ability to pay | Product fit | Evidence | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Define firmographic/behavioral qualifiers, trigger, user, champion, buyer, disqualifiers, and a starter target-account or audience-building method.

## 5. Positioning and message

Include:

- Competitive frame.
- Differentiated capability → customer value mapping.
- Positioning statement.
- Homepage headline and subhead.
- 30-second spoken pitch.
- Three proof points needed.
- Top objections with truthful responses.

Do not invent proof. Convert missing proof into an evidence-collection task.

## 6. Monetization design

Compare at least two plausible models when the answer is not obvious:

| Model | Value alignment | Predictability | Conversion friction | Margin | Operational complexity | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Recommend:

- Value metric.
- Packages and upgrade triggers.
- Price hypothesis or research range; distinguish evidence from conjecture.
- Free, trial, demo, or paid-pilot entry.
- Billing cadence and usage treatment.
- Expansion path.
- Gross-margin considerations.
- Pricing research/test plan.
- Migration needs if customers already pay.

Never present a precise unsupported price as an optimized answer.

## 7. Sales motion

Provide:

- Motion and why economics/buying process support it.
- Target-account criteria and first-list method.
- Outreach message tailored to the ICP and trigger.
- Discovery questions.
- Demo narrative.
- Qualification record.
- Pipeline stages and exit criteria.
- Offer/pilot structure.
- Objection handling.
- Mutual action plan for complex sales.
- Activation and customer-success handoff.

## 8. Marketing and distribution

Recommend one primary and no more than two supporting channels:

| Channel | Buyer behavior fit | Asset/offer | Leading metric | Time to signal | Cost/risk | Stop/continue rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Include:

- Audience/problem/message matrix.
- Landing-page outline.
- Four-week content or campaign system only for chosen channels.
- Launch/relaunch plan if relevant.
- Lifecycle triggers.
- Growth loop if the product naturally supports one.
- Brand trust and proof-building actions.

Do not produce a generic calendar for every social network.

## 9. Funnel and instrumentation

Specify:

- Entity and event for every stage.
- Activation definition and window.
- Retention interval based on natural usage.
- Revenue movement definitions.
- Segments/cohorts.
- Dashboard with owners and review cadence.
- Data-quality checks.

Show formulas for the metrics used to make decisions.

## 10. Experiment backlog

Rank 5–10 experiments. Each must identify:

| Rank | Constraint | Hypothesis | Test | Primary metric | Guardrail | Evidence/decision | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Mix customer-learning, offer, channel, activation, and monetization tests only when they address the diagnosed constraint. Avoid testing several major variables simultaneously.

## 11. Execution plan

Use a 30/60/90-day plan or a shorter stage-appropriate sequence:

| Period | Outcome | Actions | Owner | Measure | Decision unlocked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Identify dependencies and what should explicitly wait.

## 12. Risks and boundaries

Cover material risks:

- Weak or missing customer evidence.
- Product/activation/retention limits.
- Channel concentration.
- Deliverability or platform policy.
- Privacy and consent.
- Marketing claims and endorsements.
- Billing, tax, automatic-renewal, cancellation, or accounting requirements.
- Margin/cost volatility.
- Operational capacity and customer trust.

State when professional legal, tax, or accounting review is needed.

## 13. Immediate next actions

End with exactly three ordered actions:

```text
1. Action — owner — deadline
   Evidence produced:
   Decision unlocked:

2. Action — owner — deadline
   Evidence produced:
   Decision unlocked:

3. Action — owner — deadline
   Evidence produced:
   Decision unlocked:
```

Do not end with “consider,” “explore,” or an unranked list.
