# Business-Model Adaptations

Use after the initial diagnosis. Select the closest model, state why, and adapt the plan. Products can combine models, but every hybrid needs a clear acquisition path, payer, value unit, and economic model.

## Quick routing

| Model | Typical buyer | Primary motion | Common monetization | Critical metric | Frequent mistake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low-touch B2B SaaS | Individual/team lead | Self-serve or product-led | Subscription, seat, tier | Activated-account retention | Adding sales cost before contract value supports it |
| Mid-market/enterprise B2B | Department/executive | Founder-led then sales-led | Annual contract, seat, usage, platform fee | Qualified pipeline to retained ARR | Treating a long pilot as product-market fit |
| Consumer subscription | Individual | Product/brand/creator/performance | Monthly/annual subscription | Cohort contribution and retained paid use | Optimizing trial conversion while churn remains high |
| Transactional/e-commerce | Individual/business | Search, social, creator, repeat purchase | Product margin, bundle, membership | Contribution margin and repeat rate | Reporting revenue without returns, fulfillment, and media cost |
| Marketplace | Buyer and supplier | Liquidity by narrow market | Take rate, listing, lead, subscription | Match success, frequency, and contribution | Scaling one side before local/category liquidity |
| Developer/API | Developer and technical buyer | Docs, community, product-led, sales assist | Usage, credits, subscription, enterprise | Time-to-first-success and retained production use | Metering technical activity unrelated to customer value |
| AI product | User/team/enterprise | Product-led or sales-led by risk/value | Seat, usage, credit, workflow, outcome hybrid | Retained value after variable model cost | Calling experimental usage durable ARR |
| Open-source commercial | Developer/user plus organization | Community/product-led then enterprise | Cloud, support, governance, security, enterprise | Community-to-production-to-paid conversion | Gating the adoption engine before commercial value is clear |
| Services-enabled software | Business buyer | Founder-led/consultative | Setup/service plus recurring software | Contribution and path to repeatable delivery | Hiding labor inside software margin |
| Physical product | Consumer/business | Retail, direct, distributor, sales | Unit margin, bundle, consumable, service | Contribution after landed and channel cost | Ignoring inventory, returns, working capital, and channel margin |
| Local/service business | Local consumer/business | Search, referral, partnerships, outbound | Project, retainer, membership | Capacity-adjusted contribution and repeat/referral | Buying leads without response and fulfillment capacity |

## Low-touch B2B SaaS

Design around an account or workspace, even when individuals sign up.

- Activation should represent completion of the recurring team job, not profile setup.
- Marketing should target a recognizable use case and searchable problem.
- Let product behavior identify sales-assist signals such as multi-user adoption, integration needs, security interest, or high usage.
- Price against team value while avoiding seat fences that suppress collaboration.
- Track account activation, retained workspaces, free-to-paid conversion, expansion, GRR, NRR, and support cost.

## Mid-market and enterprise B2B

Account for multiple people and non-product risk.

- Map user, champion, economic buyer, technical approver, security/legal/procurement, and executive sponsor.
- Use a mutual action plan, success criteria, and decision date for evaluations.
- Price may include platform, seat, usage, services, minimum commitment, or a justified combination.
- Build proof for security, reliability, integration, implementation, ROI, and vendor durability.
- Track stage conversion, days in stage, no-decision, discount, implementation time, activation, retained ARR, and expansion.

Do not scale headcount from pipeline value alone. Inspect source, qualification, close history, capacity, and retained outcomes.

## Consumer subscription

Consumer intent and churn can change quickly.

- Identify the recurring job and its natural frequency.
- Instrument first value, habit formation, renewal exposure, voluntary cancellation, failed payments, and return behavior.
- Compare free, trial, and paid entry using cohort contribution rather than conversion alone.
- Marketing message and product onboarding must set compatible expectations.
- Price against recurring consumer value and alternatives, with transparent renewal and cancellation.

Track retained paid cohorts and contribution by source. Cheap installs can be expensive customers.

## Transactional and e-commerce

Model the complete order:

```text
Net sales
- discounts
- returns/refunds
- cost of goods
- payment fees
- pick/pack/ship and duties
- variable support/fraud
= order contribution
```

- Segment new versus returning customers.
- Use merchandising, bundles, replenishment, loyalty, content, creators, search, retail, or wholesale only when they fit purchase behavior.
- Measure incrementality and contribution after media cost, not platform-reported return on ad spend alone.
- Include inventory, cash conversion, stockouts, lead time, returns, and channel margin in growth decisions.

## Marketplace

Define the atomic unit of liquidity: a buyer request matched to acceptable supply within a useful time and geography/category.

- Start with a constrained market where both sides can become liquid.
- Identify the harder side and seed it manually when justified.
- Measure search/request-to-match, fulfillment, time-to-match, repeat rate, quality, cancellation, disintermediation, and contribution per transaction.
- Choose take rate or fees based on created value, payment flow, trust, and off-platform alternatives.
- Build trust mechanisms appropriate to risk: identity, reviews, guarantees, curation, insurance, dispute handling, or standards.

Gross marketplace volume is not company revenue.

## Developer and API products

Developers evaluate through product truth:

- Documentation, working examples, transparent limits, reliability, and debugging are marketing assets.
- Activation is a successful meaningful call, integration, deployment, or workflow—not an API key creation.
- Distribution may come from search, examples, ecosystems, integrations, community, templates, or open source.
- The user may not be the buyer. Create an organizational upgrade path for scale, governance, security, support, and procurement.
- Usage pricing needs customer-readable units, cost predictability, alerts, and auditable meters.

Track time-to-first-success, production conversion, retained usage, unit margin, account concentration, and sales-assist signals.

## AI products

Separate novelty from durable workflow value.

- Define the task or outcome that repeats after experimentation.
- Measure accepted/completed outcomes, not token consumption alone.
- Model marginal cost by workflow, model, tool call, retry, latency, storage, and high-percentile user.
- Design fallbacks and human review where errors are consequential.
- Use credits only when customers understand what a credit buys.
- Consider hybrid pricing when pure usage creates customer anxiety or volatile revenue.
- Segment trial users, experimental teams, and production customers.

Do not present gross bookings from cancellable experiments as durable recurring revenue.

## Open-source commercial products

Keep adoption value separate from organizational paid value.

- Define the community user and the enterprise buyer.
- Free/open value may drive trust, distribution, contribution, integrations, and standardization.
- Paid value commonly includes managed operation, reliability, scale, security, governance, compliance, support, or cross-team administration.
- Track meaningful active use, production adoption, cloud conversion, qualified organizational signals, retained paid accounts, and community health.
- Respect the actual software/content license in packaging and claims.

Avoid making the open version unusable solely to force conversion; it can destroy the distribution advantage.

## Services-enabled software

Decide whether service is a learning wedge, implementation requirement, permanent premium layer, or hidden product gap.

- Standardize inputs, deliverables, time, staffing, and success criteria.
- Price labor and risk explicitly.
- Track delivery hours, external cost, cycle time, rework, contribution, software adoption, and renewal.
- Productize repeated work only when repetition and customer value support it.

Do not claim software margins while recurring human work grows with every customer.

## Physical products

Connect brand and demand to operational economics.

- Define landed unit cost, packaging, freight, duties, storage, channel margin, returns, warranty, and inventory loss.
- Separate direct, retail, distributor, and marketplace economics.
- Use sampling, creators, retail, community, search, partnerships, or field sales according to discovery and trust behavior.
- Build proof through product experience, demonstrations, credible reviews, certifications, and repeat use.
- Model reorder cycle, inventory cover, cash timing, and stockout risk before scaling campaigns.

## Local and service businesses

- Target a reachable geography and high-value job.
- Optimize lead response, qualification, booking, show rate, close, delivery capacity, review/referral, and repeat purchase.
- Local search, referrals, partnerships, direct outreach, and marketplaces often outperform broad brand campaigns.
- Price by project, hour, retainer, package, membership, or outcome according to scope and risk.
- Protect service quality and capacity; demand beyond fulfillment can damage the brand.

## Hybrid-model rule

For each revenue stream answer:

```text
Who pays?
For what value?
Using which unit?
At what point in the journey?
With what gross/contribution margin?
Through which acquisition and sales motion?
What customer behavior expands it?
What operational complexity does it add?
```

Keep a hybrid only when the streams reinforce one another or serve clearly distinct segments. A collection of possible business models is not a strategy.
