# Sales System

Use for founder-led sales, outbound, inbound qualification, product-led sales, partnerships, or enterprise sales.

## Founder-led learning loop

Before delegating the motion, founders should be able to explain:

- Which accounts consistently experience the problem.
- Which trigger makes the problem urgent.
- Who feels the pain, who uses the product, and who controls budget.
- Which alternative loses and why.
- Which proof changes skepticism.
- Which objections signal a real product gap versus poor fit.
- What makes a customer activate, retain, expand, or churn.

The objective is not founder heroics forever. It is enough direct learning to create a teachable process.

## Account and contact design

Define the target account before finding contacts:

```text
Industry/use case:
Company or team size:
Technology/workflow signals:
Trigger event:
Pain hypothesis:
Likely user:
Champion:
Economic buyer:
Technical/security approver:
Disqualifiers:
```

Rank accounts by fit and timing. Do not treat an unfiltered contact database as pipeline.

## Outreach

Effective outreach earns a conversation by demonstrating relevance. A useful structure:

```text
Subject: <specific, low-hype context>

Hi <name> — noticed <observable trigger or workflow>.

Teams dealing with <specific situation> often struggle with <costly problem>.
We help <peer type> achieve <outcome> by <credible mechanism>.

Is <problem> a priority for you this quarter? If so, I can share <small useful next step>.
```

Rules:

- Personalize around business relevance, not trivia.
- Use one problem and one call to action.
- Make claims proportionate to available proof.
- Test targeting, message, offer, and channel separately.
- Track positive replies, qualified conversations, and created pipeline—not opens alone.
- Respect applicable consent, identification, opt-out, privacy, platform, and deliverability rules. Verify current rules for the recipient's jurisdiction and channel.

## Discovery

Discovery establishes whether a material problem and purchase path exist.

Ask for concrete history before opinions:

- Walk me through the last time this happened.
- What triggered the work?
- How is it handled today, by whom, and with which tools?
- How often does it happen?
- What breaks, slows down, or becomes expensive?
- What have you already tried?
- Why solve it now rather than later?
- Who else is affected or involved in a decision?
- What would a successful outcome change?
- What evidence would you need before adopting a solution?
- Is there a deadline, budget cycle, security review, or procurement step?

Avoid leading questions, feature pitching during problem discovery, and asking only whether someone “would use” an imaginary solution.

## Qualification

Use a lightweight evidence record instead of an acronym as theater:

| Dimension | Evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | Specific workflow and consequence |
| Priority | Reason to act now and competing priorities |
| Fit | Product can deliver the required outcome |
| People | User, champion, buyer, blockers, and approvers |
| Process | Evaluation, security, legal, procurement, and decision steps |
| Economics | Budget source and value relative to price |
| Next step | Named owner and date for a customer action |

An opportunity without a customer-owned next step is not progressing.

## Demo

Use this order:

1. Restate the customer's current workflow and desired outcome.
2. Confirm what matters in this session.
3. Show the shortest product path to that outcome.
4. Use the customer's data or realistic scenario when permitted.
5. Pause for reaction and questions.
6. Address risk with proof, limitations, and implementation detail.
7. Agree on a mutual next step.

Do not tour every feature. A demo should make the purchase decision easier.

## Pipeline stages

Customize stages, but require objective exit criteria:

| Stage | Exit criterion |
| --- | --- |
| Target | Account matches observable ICP and trigger |
| Engaged | Buyer or relevant stakeholder responds |
| Discovery | Current workflow, pain, priority, and people documented |
| Qualified | Fit, process, economics, and dated next step confirmed |
| Evaluation | Customer is performing an agreed product or proof step |
| Proposal | Scope, commercial terms, and decision process delivered |
| Commit | Decision path is complete; remaining actions are explicit |
| Won | Binding agreement/payment as appropriate |
| Activated | Customer reaches the agreed first-value milestone |
| Retained | Customer repeats the value behavior in the expected cycle |

Track lost reason and no-decision separately. “No decision” often means insufficient urgency, weak differentiation, or an unaddressed process.

## Offer and proof

Reduce risk without giving away unlimited labor:

- Paid diagnostic or workshop for complex discovery.
- Time-bound pilot with success criteria, stakeholder, data access, and decision date.
- Narrow initial scope with a defined expansion path.
- Relevant case study structured as situation → intervention → observable result.
- Security, implementation, or ROI material tailored to the buyer's actual risk.

A pilot without success criteria and a commercial next step is usually extended presales.

## Objections

Classify before answering:

- **No pain:** disqualify or return to discovery.
- **No priority:** identify trigger or stop pursuing.
- **No trust:** supply appropriate proof or reduce implementation risk.
- **No differentiation:** sharpen alternative and value comparison.
- **No budget:** change scope, buyer, timing, or economic case; do not reflexively discount.
- **Missing capability:** determine whether it is required for the segment or a one-off request.
- **Process risk:** address security, legal, procurement, integration, or change management.

Confirm the objection is real: “If we resolved this, is anything else preventing a decision?”

## Closing and implementation

Use a mutual action plan for multi-step sales:

```text
Business outcome:
Success measure:
Stakeholders:
Evaluation tasks and owners:
Security/legal/procurement tasks:
Commercial decision date:
Target launch date:
Activation milestone:
First value review:
```

Do not use artificial urgency. Real urgency comes from the customer's trigger, opportunity cost, deadline, or risk.

## Customer success and expansion

The sale is incomplete until value is reached. Monitor:

- Time-to-first-value and activation completion.
- Adoption of the behavior that predicts retention.
- Champion engagement and stakeholder coverage.
- Support friction and unresolved implementation work.
- Outcome review before renewal.
- Expansion signal tied to more customer value: users, workflows, volume, business units, or products.

Never disguise an arbitrary price increase as expansion.

## Hiring the motion

Hire sales capacity after the founders can provide:

- A recognizable ICP and reachable account list.
- Repeatable discovery and demo patterns.
- Credible proof and common objection handling.
- Defined stages with conversion history.
- Contract value and cycle compatible with the role's cost.
- Enough qualified opportunity volume for the hire to learn.

Do not ask the first salesperson to simultaneously discover the market, define the product, invent positioning, generate every lead, and close an unknown motion.
