# Marketing System

Use for positioning, messaging, demand creation, acquisition, launches, content, lifecycle, and growth loops.

## Marketing's job

Marketing should make a well-matched customer:

1. Recognize a costly problem or desired progress.
2. Understand why the product is relevant.
3. Believe the product can deliver.
4. Take the next appropriate action.
5. Arrive with expectations the product can fulfill.

Traffic that cannot activate, buy, or retain is not successful demand generation.

## Positioning and message

Build the message from evidence in this order:

```text
For <specific customer in a triggering situation>,
who currently <alternative or workaround>,
<product> is a <useful category/frame>
that helps them <valuable outcome>.
Unlike <main alternative>,
it <differentiated capability and mechanism>.
Evidence: <proof>.
```

This is an internal positioning statement, not necessarily public copy.

Create a message hierarchy:

- **Problem:** recognizable situation in customer language.
- **Promise:** important outcome, not a vague superlative.
- **Mechanism:** how the product creates the outcome.
- **Difference:** why the current alternative cannot provide the same combination.
- **Proof:** demonstration, result, expert validation, adoption, or transparent evidence.
- **Action:** next step appropriate to buyer readiness.

Avoid “all-in-one,” “revolutionary,” “seamless,” or “AI-powered” unless the words convey a concrete buying reason.

## Category and awareness

Match communication to how the audience understands the problem:

| Audience state | Marketing task |
| --- | --- |
| Unaware | Show the costly situation, not the product category |
| Problem-aware | Explain stakes, causes, and approaches |
| Solution-aware | Clarify the mechanism and category |
| Product-aware | Establish difference, proof, and fit |
| Most aware | Make offer, price, risk, and action clear |

Category creation requires more education and capital than entering an understood category. Use a new category only when existing frames materially hide the product's value.

## Channel selection

Choose channels from buyer behavior, product economics, and speed of learning:

| Channel | Strong when | Weak when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder-led outbound | Narrow identifiable accounts, expensive pain | Audience cannot be identified or contract value is tiny |
| Search/content | Existing problem or solution demand | Category has no search language or value is highly visual/impulsive |
| Community | Audience gathers around a shared practice | Participation is extractive or community trust is absent |
| Partnerships | Complementary provider already has trust/distribution | Incentives and ownership are unclear |
| Product-led/referral | Product value is fast, repeatable, and shareable | Setup is complex or value is private/rare |
| Social/creator | Buyer learns through people and demonstrations | Content cannot credibly connect to product demand |
| Paid acquisition | Conversion, retention, margin, and payback are measurable | Funnel economics are unknown or weak |
| Events/field | Buyers value trust, peer proof, and concentrated access | Contract value cannot support cost and follow-up |
| PR | News creates credibility or category awareness | Used as a substitute for continuous distribution |

Select one primary channel and at most two supporting channels for the first plan. State why each fits, its leading metric, cost/risk, time-to-signal, and stopping rule.

## Content strategy

Build content around customer decisions rather than a posting quota:

- **Problem content:** symptoms, cost, diagnosis, and common failure modes.
- **Approach content:** frameworks, workflows, comparisons, and tradeoffs.
- **Proof content:** demonstrations, benchmarks, case studies, and teardown evidence.
- **Product content:** use cases, integration guides, implementation, and release education.
- **Decision content:** alternatives, pricing logic, security, ROI, and stakeholder material.

Each item should serve a named audience state, distribution channel, next action, and measurable hypothesis. Repurpose only when the format fits the channel.

For search, favor original, people-first material that satisfies a real query. Do not mass-produce derivative pages, stuff keywords, or promise nonexistent answers. Verify current Google Search guidance before technical or policy-sensitive recommendations.

## Landing page architecture

Use only sections supported by the product and audience:

1. Audience-relevant outcome and category.
2. Problem recognition and stakes.
3. Product mechanism shown through the workflow.
4. Differentiated value versus the real alternative.
5. Proof near the claim it supports.
6. Use cases or segments with distinct needs.
7. Integration, security, or adoption details where they block purchase.
8. Packaging and pricing or a clear commercial next step.
9. Objections and frequently asked questions.
10. One primary call to action.

Do not hide the product behind a cinematic brand story when buyers need to understand what it does.

## Launch design

A launch is a concentrated distribution event, not the complete growth system.

Before launch:

- Define the target audience and desired action.
- Confirm onboarding, activation measurement, support capacity, and payment flow.
- Recruit relevant early users and collect truthful proof.
- Prepare channel-native assets and direct outreach.
- Establish baseline metrics and a follow-up sequence.

During launch:

- Demonstrate the product and why it matters now.
- Respond to questions and capture objections.
- Segment traffic and conversion by source.
- Avoid fabricated votes, reviews, urgency, or engagement.

After launch:

- Personally help qualified users reach value.
- Compare activated and retained cohorts, not only visits or signups.
- Turn recurring questions into product and content improvements.
- Continue the channel that produces qualified, retained customers.

## Growth loops

A loop has an output that creates the next input:

```text
New or returning user
  → receives product value
  → creates an artifact, invitation, result, or story
  → reaches a relevant potential user
  → potential user enters and receives value
```

Examples include collaboration, user-generated artifacts, referrals, marketplaces, integrations, data contribution, and customer proof. Specify loop trigger, carrier, recipient, conversion event, cycle time, and failure point.

Do not call a one-way campaign a loop.

## Lifecycle marketing

Trigger communication from customer state:

- Incomplete setup: help finish the next meaningful step.
- Activated: reinforce the achieved value and next use case.
- Stalled: diagnose friction rather than sending generic reminders.
- Approaching limit: explain value, usage, and options before the bill changes.
- Expansion signal: connect additional purchase to additional outcome.
- Churn risk: address the underlying adoption, value, service, or payment issue.
- Churned: capture reason, preserve data expectations, and offer a relevant return path.

Measure incremental customer behavior. Email sends and opens are delivery diagnostics, not business outcomes.

## Brand and trust

Brand is the repeated expectation associated with the product. Build it through:

- A useful product and reliable experience.
- A clear promise and recognizable point of view.
- Consistent product, marketing, sales, support, and pricing behavior.
- Distinctive verbal and visual assets.
- Credible proof and transparent limitations.
- Customer rituals, language, stories, and community participation that emerge from real value.

Do not use brand work to postpone customer contact or obscure a weak product.

## Marketing review

Review weekly:

- Qualified reach and demand by segment/source.
- Message response and leading conversion.
- Activation and early retention by acquisition source.
- Cost and time invested per channel.
- Customer language, objections, and content gaps.
- Experiments completed and decisions made.

Review monthly:

- Cohort retention and revenue by source/segment.
- Pipeline or self-serve revenue contribution.
- Channel concentration and saturation risk.
- Whether the ICP, message, or primary constraint changed.
