# Product and Revenue Diagnosis

Use this reference before recommending channels, sales tactics, or pricing.

## Build the evidence ledger

Capture what is known and label the basis:

| Item | What to establish | Evidence examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | What it does and the shortest path to value | Product, README, demo, onboarding |
| User | Who operates the product | Usage data, interviews, support conversations |
| Buyer | Who approves or pays | Contracts, checkout data, sales notes |
| Job | Progress the customer is trying to make | Customer language and observed workflow |
| Pain | Frequency, severity, urgency, and consequence | Existing spend, manual work, incidents, deadlines |
| Alternative | What happens without this product | Competitor, spreadsheet, service, internal tool, inaction |
| Outcome | Customer value in observable terms | Time saved, revenue gained, risk reduced, quality improved |
| Proof | Why a skeptical buyer should believe | Demo, case study, cohort behavior, testimonial, benchmark |
| Friction | What blocks evaluation, purchase, or adoption | Funnel data, objections, abandonment, implementation time |
| Economics | Price, cost to serve, acquisition cost, margin | Billing, infrastructure, labor, channel costs |

Do not convert an unverified product claim into a fact. Write “the product claims,” “the user reports,” or “I infer” where appropriate.

## Determine the stage

### Pre-revenue

Evidence is mostly qualitative. Optimize for problem truth, willingness to engage, and willingness to pay. Prefer founder conversations, concierge delivery, paid pilots, and narrow audiences. Do not forecast scale from clicks or compliments.

### Early revenue

Some customers pay, but the motion is not repeatable. Optimize for a sharper ICP, consistent qualification, shorter time-to-value, objection patterns, pricing evidence, and retention by cohort. Founders should remain close to sales.

### Repeatable motion

A segment, channel, offer, and delivery system show repeated results. Optimize pipeline coverage, conversion by stage, sales cycle, onboarding, payback, retention, and expansion. Document the motion before adding headcount.

### Scale

Multiple cohorts and channels have credible economics. Optimize channel saturation, segmentation, sales capacity, pricing governance, internationalization, operational controls, and portfolio-level resource allocation.

Stage is specific to a segment and motion. A company can have a repeatable SMB motion and an unproven enterprise motion.

## Locate the binding constraint

Map the journey and find the first transition that is both weak and consequential:

```text
Eligible audience
  → Reached
  → Engaged
  → Qualified
  → Started evaluation
  → Activated
  → Paid
  → Retained
  → Expanded or referred
```

Common diagnostic patterns:

| Signal | Likely constraint | Check before acting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Few qualified visitors | Awareness/distribution | Is the ICP narrow and reachable? |
| Traffic but weak response | Positioning/message | Does the page name pain, outcome, audience, and proof? |
| Replies but few meetings | Offer or qualification | Is the ask appropriate and the pain urgent? |
| Meetings but few evaluations | Discovery/demo | Is the product mapped to a costly workflow? |
| Signups but little value reached | Activation | What behavior predicts retained use? |
| Activation but weak payment | Packaging, price, buyer, or paywall | Are value and purchase authority aligned? |
| Payment but rapid churn | Value durability or expectation mismatch | Do retained ICP cohorts behave differently? |
| Strong retention but flat revenue | Acquisition or expansion | Is there a reachable channel or natural growth axis? |
| Failed payments | Billing operations | Separate involuntary from voluntary churn |

Do not infer the bottleneck from a single blended conversion rate. Segment by ICP, source, plan, cohort, geography, device, and sales motion when sample size allows.

## Select a motion

Use customer economics and buying complexity:

| Conditions | Likely motion |
| --- | --- |
| Low price, individual user, immediate value | Self-serve/product-led |
| Self-serve adoption with team or security upgrade | Product-led sales |
| New category, unproven message, accessible buyers | Founder-led sales |
| Observable accounts with costly pain and adequate contract value | Targeted outbound |
| Existing search demand and educational buying journey | Inbound/content/search |
| High contract value, many stakeholders, procurement/security | Enterprise sales |
| Buyer trusts an intermediary or implementation partner | Channel/partner |
| Value depends on matching supply and demand | Marketplace liquidity motion |

A hybrid needs a handoff rule. Example: self-serve until a workspace has five active users or requests security review, then product-led sales.

## ICP quality test

An initial ICP should have:

- Observable membership: accounts can be identified before purchase.
- Shared painful job: not merely shared demographics.
- Trigger: a change or deadline that creates urgency.
- Reachability: at least one credible path to the buyer.
- Ability and authority to pay.
- Similar onboarding and value delivery.
- Enough density for learning, but not a definition such as “all small businesses.”

Write explicit disqualifiers. A segment becomes operational when a founder knows whom not to pursue.

## Positioning sequence

1. Competitive alternatives, including manual work and doing nothing.
2. Capabilities customers cannot get from those alternatives.
3. Customer value created by those capabilities.
4. Segment that cares most about that value.
5. Category or frame that makes the value understandable.
6. Proof that reduces perceived risk.

Only then write the headline, pitch, or tagline.
